Two rounds of critique settled most of the direction. Four questions are left, and none of them can be settled by argument — three directors already tried. Try each option with your thumb, mark what you prefer, and send me the tally at the bottom.

Every figure below is real, from a seeded month at your scale. Inside each test only one thing changes — everything else is held constant on purpose.

Test 01 · home control

Sliding cards, a tick strip, or both?

Settled already: home shows one figure, and what you move is the time horizon — Today, Week, Month, Year. Both directors got there independently. What isn't settled is how you move it.

The tangle Motion built your swipe-cards, then conceded them — the old card order was a shuffle, so you could never predict what was to the right. UX invented the strip, then withdrew it. They swapped. Both options below are honest and both order the axis, so doctrine doesn't decide this. Your thumb does.
A · Sliding cards Your mechanic. Drag the card away and the next horizon arrives. Peers peek at the edge.
B · Tick strip The figure stays put and re-states. Drag the strip, or tap a tick. The whole axis is visible at rest.
C · Both Cards slide over a strip that also works. Drag either. My candidate — no director has vetted it.
Watch for Whether you can tell what's to the right before you get there, and whether the number ever shows a value that isn't real. It shouldn't — figures step, they never count.
Test 02 · the bar ramp

Is the colour carrying the fraction, or the culture?

You asked for the warm ramp, and the reasoning is in bar-grammar.ts. Creative withdrew its proposed fix once it read that — but narrowed its objection to the four hex values: green→amber→red is a verdict vocabulary, and in dark mode the calm green is the brightest thing on screen.

The test Same stops, same curve, different hues. If the bars still read correctly in every ramp, the colour was carrying fraction and the current hues are fine. If one stops working, that ramp's colour was doing the talking. Four real categories, 19% to 94% spent.
Toggle the ramp Current — green through amber to red.
Then decide Which should ship? Current keeps what you asked for. Rotated proves the point and picks new hues. Density is creative's proposal — one ink, darkening.
Test 03 · ground

Light or dark by default?

Three of your four references — Monzo, iMessage, Airbnb — are light-first. The dark one, Trading 212, is the one you like least. Creative committed to light and argued it properly: on a dark ground every accent glows, and glow reads as alarm in an app forbidden from using fear.

Caveat This is today's palette on a light ground, not creative's proposed one. It answers "does light suit this product?", not "is that the right cream". The full palette is in the specimen file.

Test 04 · the name

Does it survive being worn?

A name reads differently in a sentence than in a list. Creative recommends Spare — the app's own word for the number it exists to produce, and the exact antonym of "maximise". Its case against Max: maximise is optimisation vocabulary aimed at someone whose problem is avoidance.

Known snag UX flagged it: "spare" is already a label in the UI — "genuinely spare", "spare on 30 Aug". The name would shadow a word the product already uses for a specific figure.
